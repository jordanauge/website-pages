MAS-Lab: A Specification-Driven Validation Framework for Reliable Multi-Agent Systems

The rapid emergence of LLM-based agentic frameworks has significantly reduced the cost of assembling multi-agent systems (MAS), enabling fast prototyping and exploration of agentic behaviors. However, systems built with current tooling remain ill-suited for reliable, evolvable, and production-grade deployment. In practice, MAS are often developed in an ad-hoc and imperative manner, with agent logic, orchestration, observability, and control tightly interwoven, little to no explicit system-level validation, and development workflows optimized for demonstrations rather than long-lived, governed operation. We introduce MAS-Lab, a specification-driven framework for principled development and experimental validation of multi-agent systems properties. MAS-Lab separates semantic intent from operational concerns through three layers: a declarative agentic specification layer (Spec), a stateful MAS Operating System (MAS-OS), and lab overlays with integrated observability and evaluation tools (Labs). Together, these components enable intent-based validation, principled system evolution, and a seamless transition to production-grade MAS.

Aether: Network Validation Using Agentic AI and Digital Twin

We introduce Aether, a framework for network validation that combines agentic AI with a network digital twin. The framework enables operators to express high-level validation intents in natural language, which are then autonomously decomposed into executable verification plans by a multi-agent system. The agents interact with a digital twin of the network to run configuration checks, simulate topology changes, and verify compliance with operational policies. We demonstrate Aether on realistic network scenarios derived from production ISP deployments, showing that the approach can identify configuration errors, predict the impact of planned changes, and accelerate troubleshooting workflows. Our results suggest that combining LLM-based planning with faithful network simulation provides a practical path toward autonomous network assurance.

Measurement-Based Admission Control for Flow-Aware Implicit Service Differentiation

It has previously been shown that the combined use of fair queuing and admission control would allow the Internet to provide satisfactory quality of service for both streaming and elastic flows without explicitly identifying traffic classes. In this paper we discuss the design of the required measurement based admission control (MBAC) scheme. The context is different to that of previous work on MBAC in that there is no prior knowledge of flow characteristics and there is a twofold objective: to maintain adequate throughput for elastic flows and to ensure low packet latency for any flow whose peak rate is less than a given threshold. In the paper we consider the second objective assuming realistically that most elastic and streaming flows are rate limited. We propose an MBAC algorithm and evaluate its performance by simulation under different stationary traffic mixes and in a flash crowd scenario. The algorithm is shown to offer a satisfactory compromise between flow performance and link utilization.
